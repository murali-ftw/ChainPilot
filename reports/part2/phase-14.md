## 2. Per use case

Full per-arm rows are in `ml/artifacts/phase14_scores.json` for both threshold objectives (F1 primary, MCC
pre-registered secondary). Confusion matrices are for seed 7 on test. Every τ was fitted on validation by maximising

### 2.1 UC1 — arrival lateness (contracted-lead reference; the adopted as-of reference is secondary)

**Censoring: 17,466 of 36,000 test lines (48.5%) are censored.** A censored line has T ≥ 13 weeks. So when its
reference R < 13 it is **known late**: that holds for 16,594 of them (95.0%). Three treatments, each fitted and
scored separately:

| treatment | rows | late base rate | h⁴ F1 | h⁰ F1 | b5flat_reg F1 | channel-constant ranker F1 | always-late F1 | h⁴ MCC | b5flat MCC |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** excluded | 18,534 | 0.489 | 0.674 [0.660, 0.680] | 0.671 | **0.732** [0.731, 0.733] | 0.732 | 0.657 | 0.283 | **0.404** |
| **B** censored = not late | 36,000 | 0.252 | 0.421 [0.415, 0.425] | 0.422 | 0.545 | **0.551** | 0.402 | 0.136 | 0.361 |
| **C resolved** (principal) | 35,128 | **0.730** | 0.844 [0.843, 0.844] | 0.844 | 0.847 | **0.850** | **0.844** | 0.049 | 0.128 |

- **B is wrong in the pessimistic direction.** It labels 16,594 known-late lines as on time, so every F1 falls to
  about 0.42–0.55.
- **A is the flattering choice.** It drops exactly the lines that are late by definition.
- **Under C, 73% of lines are late, and F1 is maximised by calling almost everything late.** The h⁴ confusion matrix
  at the F1 threshold is TP 25,528 / FP 9,336 / TN 140 / FN 124. No arm beats always-late disjointly.
- **At the pre-registered MCC threshold under C**, h⁴ trades recall for precision: precision 0.861 [0.836, 0.889],
  recall 0.431, MCC 0.220, accuracy **0.532, against a majority of 0.730**.

**The channel-constant ranker, which ranks by the channel's contracted term alone, ties or beats every model on
this decision.** Treatment A: F1 0.732, MCC 0.405, equal to LightGBM, and above h⁴'s 0.674 / 0.283. Lateness
*against a contract* is mostly a property of which channel the line is on. The hazard head's advantage on the
adopted as-of metric (Phase 11C) **does not carry over** to this decision.

**Adopted as-of reference (secondary):** the same pattern. Resolved: ranker F1 0.845 against always-late 0.841.
Excluded: b5flat 0.691, ranker 0.689, h⁴ 0.649.

## 3. UC5's two confusion matrices — the cells that moved

See §2.6. In one line: **of 12,401 part-plant-weeks the planners rescued, the simulation had flagged 34%.** Those
flip from false alarms to hits once the rescue is credited. **66% it had not flagged**, and those flip from correct
rejections to misses. So crediting the rescues raises precision (0.205 → 0.238) and F1 (0.286 → 0.321, disjoint),
but not recall (0.469 → 0.491).

## Plain language — what a planner can read off each decision

- **Arrival lateness against the contract.** It is right about 74 times in 100 when it says "late" and catches
  almost every late line. But **73 in 100 lines are late anyway**, so it is no better than assuming every line will
  be late. **Not useful as a yes/no.**
- **"Will arrive within 4 weeks."** It is right about **16 times in 100** when it says yes, and catches **53%** of
  the lines that do arrive that fast. Only 3 in 100 lines do, and it raises many false hopes along the way.
- **"Will arrive in full."** It says yes to almost every line and is right 75 times in 100. That is **exactly as
  often as assuming every line arrives in full**. **Not useful as a yes/no.**
- **"Will be materially short" (below 95%).** It is right about **31 times in 100** and catches **65%** of short
  lines, against 24 in 100 lines being short. It flags three lines for every one that is actually short.
- **"Demand will exceed this supplier's capacity over 90 days."** It is right about **59 times in 100** and catches
  **70%** of the real cases, against 41 in 100 at random. **It is the strongest decision here.**
- **"This part-plant is at risk of a shortage."** It is right **63 times in 100** and catches **54%**. That is
  diagnostic only: this world has about 8× the real shortage rate.
- **"This week will fall below safety stock."** It is right about **21 times in 100** and catches **47%** of the
  weeks that actually do, against 8 in 100 at random. Counting the weeks planners rescued by moving stock, it is
  right 24 times in 100.
- **Delivery schedule.** Nothing to score until the four cost figures are supplied.
- **Supplier allocation.** On the half of part-plants where any change is even feasible, the recommended top
  supplier is the one actually used **67 times in 100**. **Keeping the current supplier does just as well (65).**
- **Transfer donor.** When it names a donor plant, that plant did ship stock out that week **56 times in 100**,
  against 52–53 for "nearest" or "random". But half of all plants were shipping out anyway, and it measures
  **agreement with what planners did, not shortages avoided.**
