# v2 — Dataset Changes to Check, and What Each One Would Let Us Conclude

**Purpose.** Every layer of this project (Layer 1 architecture selection, Layer 2 depth
selection, Layer 3 hidden-dependency discovery, Step 8 dyadic reweighting) ended with at least
one open question that v1's dataset — by construction, not by any modeling failure — cannot
resolve. This document catalogs those open questions, and for each one, proposes a specific,
deliberate change to `db/generate_dataset.py` / the live database that would let v2 actually
answer it. Every entry below follows the same shape: **what we couldn't conclude in v1**, **why
v1's dataset specifically prevents concluding it**, and **the inconsistency to inject and why
it would resolve the question**.

**A note on the word "inconsistency."** Every change below is a deliberate, documented,
label-relevant structural or statistical change to the synthetic dataset — not noise for its
own sake. Each one exists because v1's dataset is currently too clean, too small, or too
structurally redundant in a specific, identified way to let a specific claim be tested to
completion. "Inconsistency" here means "a pattern the model has to actually work to detect,"
mirroring how `H_POLYMER`, `COPARENT_COUPLING`, and the four thin relations already work in
v1 — this project's whole synthetic-data philosophy has always been to build in exactly the
patterns needed to test a claim, honestly, not to fabricate an the answer in advance.

---

## 1. Give Transformer 2 a hidden dependency that is actually causally coupled

**What v1 couldn't conclude.** Whether smarter fusion (Confidence-Aware, Trust Gate,
Cross-Attention — `reports/phase3.md`) can ever turn a discovered hidden dependency into an
AUC improvement. All three fusion redesigns produced the same null result, and it was traced
to a dataset property, not a modeling one.

**Why v1's dataset specifically blocks this.** `H_POLYMER` (the only planted hidden-dependency
scenario) is real and discoverable (Transformer 2 finds it, validated decisively, every seed)
but **structurally redundant** — each member's `own_stress` already fully captures its own
H_POLYMER exposure, and the only cross-supplier bleed-through term in the whole generator
(`COPARENT_COUPLING`) is wired exclusively to Task 3's `component_suppliers` dual-sourcing
mechanism, never to H_POLYMER membership. There is nothing incrementally predictive to fuse in,
no matter how the fusion mechanism is built.

**The inconsistency to inject.** Add a new coupling parameter, e.g. `H_POLYMER_COUPLING`
(mirroring `COPARENT_COUPLING`'s own mechanics exactly), so that `stress(sup_id)` for an
H_POLYMER member includes a term derived from its *co-members'* current stress, not just its
own history — genuinely undiscoverable from the member's own features alone, exactly the shape
Claim 3 (`docs/01_Product_Requirement_Document.md` §4, row 3) actually describes: "correlated
risk that can exist between suppliers with no recorded edge, and can be surfaced without one."
**What this would let v2 conclude:** whether Confidence-Aware Fusion (or any of the three
tested designs) produces a real, sign-consistent AUC gain once there is something genuine to
find — the actual test Claim 3's validation route (`10_AI_ML_Documentation.md` §9.4) was always
meant to run, which v1's dataset never permitted.

## 2. Increase overall label volume

**What we couldn't conclude in v1.** Whether SHARE genuinely ties HGT on impact or narrowly
loses/wins (Layer 1); whether any Layer 2 depth mechanism actually helps shortage/impact or is
truly depth-indifferent; whether any Layer 3 fusion variant would separate from its baselines
given enough statistical power. Nearly every "FLIPS (noise)" verdict across all three layers
traces back to the same root cause.

**Why v1's dataset specifically blocks this.** `training_labels` sits at roughly 1,774 rows
(~11% delay, ~7% shortage, ~4% impact positive rate per `db/README.md`). At 200–1,000
positives, this project's own documented 95% CI width is ±0.017–0.037 AUC — wider than most of
the deltas being tested. "Not distinguishable at this label volume" has been the single most
repeated verdict across all three layers, and it is a property of sample size, not evidence
that the compared mechanisms are actually equivalent.

**The inconsistency to inject.** Extend the synthetic dataset's time range and/or supplier/
product/order population size (more snapshots, more entities, or both) specifically to grow
`training_labels` into the 2,000–5,000-positive range per task, tightening the CI to
±0.010–0.015 — small enough to actually separate the point estimates already observed (e.g.
Variant A's consistent +0.001–0.002 nominal edge over Markov on every task). **What this would
let v2 conclude:** whether the many "flips sign, no reliable difference" verdicts across Layer
1/2/3 are real ties, or real (small) effects that v1 simply never had the statistical power to
detect.

## 3. Root-cause SHARK's impact instability

**What we couldn't conclude in v1.** Why SHARK's impact AUC has a std an order of magnitude
wider than SHARE/SHARP's (0.0559 vs. 0.0058/0.0071), driven by one outlier seed. `reports/info.md`
Layer 1 §6 states this plainly as unexplained beyond "more expressive, more overfitting
exposure."

**Why v1's dataset specifically blocks this.** SHARK's per-relation bilinear attention matrices
are most exposed on the four data-thin relations (`SUPPLIES`, `USED_IN`, `STOCKED_AT`,
`MANUFACTURED_AT`) — but v1 has no controlled way to isolate whether the instability comes from
thin-relation overfitting specifically, versus some other source (initialization, a specific
supplier subpopulation, seed-dependent optimization dynamics unrelated to relation sparsity).

**The inconsistency to inject.** Add a controlled variant of the dataset with one or two of the
four thin relations deliberately densified (more edges, same underlying structure) while
holding everything else constant, and retrain SHARK across seeds on both the original and
densified versions. **What this would let v2 conclude:** whether SHARK's instability collapses
once the thin-relation overfitting surface shrinks (confirming the theory) or persists
regardless (pointing to a different, currently unidentified cause).

## 4. Test whether shortage/impact are genuinely depth-indifferent

**What we couldn't conclude in v1.** Layer 2's open items state plainly: five independent
depth-selection mechanisms, five different designs, the same negative result on shortage and
impact specifically — is that because these tasks are truly depth-indifferent (no depth choice
matters), or because v1's dataset doesn't vary chain length enough to make depth matter?

**Why v1's dataset specifically blocks this.** The current BOM chain length (Supplier→
Component→Product→...→Order→Customer) is structurally fixed — every product's chain is roughly
the same number of hops, so there's no natural variation in "how far away is the signal" for a
depth mechanism to actually have to detect and respond to.

**The inconsistency to inject.** Introduce genuine chain-length heterogeneity — some products
with a shorter BOM chain (fewer intermediate tiers) and some with a deliberately longer one
(an extra tier of sub-components), so that the *correct* depth for shortage/impact genuinely
varies by product rather than being uniform across the dataset. **What this would let v2
conclude:** whether a per-node depth mechanism (Rung 5 / Variant A) starts showing a real,
sign-consistent AUC benefit once there is genuine per-node depth variation to exploit — the
scenario these mechanisms were originally built for but v1 never actually presented them with.

## 5. Test the "lower-degree nodes deviate more" pattern directly

**What we couldn't conclude in v1.** Variants B/C/D and the original Rung 5 all independently
found that nodes deviating from the Markov depth prior were consistently *lower*-degree — the
opposite of the theory document's original "hub suppliers deviate" hypothesis. This replicated
four times but was never causally tested — only observed.

**Why v1's dataset specifically blocks this.** Supplier degree in v1 is not independently
manipulable from other node properties (component count, disruption history, etc.) — so a
correlation between low degree and depth-gate deviation can't be distinguished from a
correlation with whatever else co-varies with low degree in the current generator.

**The inconsistency to inject.** Generate a controlled cohort of synthetic suppliers whose
degree is deliberately randomized independent of every other property (disruption rate,
component count, tier), so degree becomes an isolable variable. **What this would let v2
conclude:** whether low degree itself causes gate deviation, or whether the earlier
observations were confounded by something degree happens to correlate with in v1's organically
generated population.

## 6. Grow `fulfilment_preference_weight` coverage beyond 6.7%

**What we couldn't conclude in v1.** Step 8's double-counting test passed at the aggregate
level, but the lead signal (`fulfilment_preference_weight` — the one input the encoder
structurally cannot see) only has real evidence for 1,923 of 28,529 (6.7%) customer-supplier
pairs. The claim "Claim B leads on the encoder-blind signal" is only literally exercised for a
small minority of scored pairs today.

**Why v1's dataset specifically blocks this.** The signal requires a supplier to have
experienced at least `min_concurrent_delayed=2` simultaneously-constrained weeks within a
90-day trailing window — a fairly demanding co-occurrence condition against v1's current
disruption-event density.

**The inconsistency to inject.** Increase the frequency and overlap of supplier-side
disruption/delay events specifically (more concurrent-constraint weeks per supplier, not just
more disruptions overall), targeting majority coverage (>50%) of customer-supplier pairs having
a computable `fulfilment_preference_weight`. **What this would let v2 conclude:** whether Claim
B's independence result (reordering rate ratio 0.9898) holds, strengthens, or weakens once the
lead signal is actually driving the majority of scored pairs, rather than a 6.7% minority
propped up by the other two (fully-covered) inputs.

## 7. Persist an HGT checkpoint to make the SHARE-vs-HGT comparison paired

**What we couldn't conclude in v1.** `reports/info.md` Layer 1 §6 flags that SHARE's win over
HGT on impact remains a statistical tie, not a confirmed win, and that every comparison against
HGT has been **unpaired**, since no HGT checkpoint has ever been persisted in this codebase (a
deliberate standing rule — every comparison retrains fresh, in-process).

**Why v1's dataset specifically blocks this.** This is not strictly a dataset change — it's a
governance gap that compounds with v1's already-limited label volume (item 2 above): an
unpaired comparison has strictly less statistical power than a paired one at the same sample
size, on top of the wide CIs item 2 already documents.

**The inconsistency to inject — a process change, not a data change, included here because it
compounds directly with the label-volume fix above.** Introduce a single, explicitly-versioned
exception to the no-checkpoint rule: persist one canonical HGT checkpoint (same seeds, same
snapshot split) purely as a frozen comparison anchor, never retrained, clearly labeled as an
exception in `model_registry`. **What this would let v2 conclude:** whether SHARE's tie with
HGT on impact is a genuine tie or an artifact of comparing against five freshly-retrained HGT
seeds' own noise on top of SHARE's — a confound the current unpaired setup cannot separate out.

## 8. Explain the Shipment anti-smoothing pattern

**What we couldn't conclude in v1.** All three Layer 1 hybrids (SHARE/SHARP/SHARK)
independently reproduce the same anti-smoothing pattern on Shipment embeddings — similarity
*falling* with depth, the opposite of every other node type — flagged as "an
architecture-independent property of the graph, not a model artifact," but never root-caused.

**Why v1's dataset specifically blocks this.** Shipment nodes sit at a specific structural
position (leaf-like, high fan-out from Supplier/Factory, low fan-in) that isn't independently
varied anywhere in v1 — there's no controlled comparison to isolate which structural property
(fan-out, position in the graph, feature sparsity) actually drives the anti-smoothing effect.

**The inconsistency to inject.** Construct a synthetic node-type variant with Shipment's same
structural position (leaf-like, high out-degree from Supplier/Factory) but different feature
characteristics, and a second variant with different structural position but similar features
— a 2×2 isolation design. **What this would let v2 conclude:** whether the anti-smoothing
pattern is driven by Shipment's graph position specifically (motivating a structural fix) or by
its feature characteristics (motivating a feature-engineering fix) — currently indistinguishable
from a single, non-manipulated node type.

## How to use this document

Each entry is independently actionable — none blocks any other, and none requires reversing any
v1 result to test. Per this project's own standing discipline (test one isolated change at a
time, paired bootstrap + 5-seed sign-consistency, report negative results plainly), each v2
change should be built and evaluated on its own, against the same v1 baseline it's meant to
clarify, not stacked with the others. Suggested priority, given `docs/01_Product_Requirement_Document.md`
§4's own claim ordering (Claim 1 core, Claims 2–4 as time and evidence allow): item 2 (label
volume) first, since it compounds with nearly every other open question in this document; item
1 (T2 causal coupling) second, since it is the only item blocking an entire claim (Claim 3) from
ever being conclusively tested one way or the other, regardless of how much fusion-design effort
is spent on it.
